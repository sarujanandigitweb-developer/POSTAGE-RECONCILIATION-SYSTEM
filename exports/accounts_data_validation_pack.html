Before you review: the source tables changed five times today

While this data was being fetched, the rate-card tables were rebuilt (blos.postage was dropped and split into blos.local_postage + blos.international_postage), a vat_rate_percentage column was added, and public.carrier_service — a 380-row carrier lookup table — was dropped entirely.

Separately, week W27 is not frozen. Orders are still being back-dated into it: total cost moved from £12,517.31 to £12,527.90 over three days, and the most recent order stamped inside W27 was created on 10 July, five days after the week closed. Please treat every £ figure as “as-of 10 July”, not final.

How to read this pack

Value means PostgreSQL supplied it directly. Conflict means two readings are possible and we need your ruling. Unavailable means no column exists in the database — it is not a blank we forgot to fill, and it must not be read as zero.

Three findings that need a decision, not a fix

The single most common “carrier” is the empty string — 231,875 labels (21% of the table) have carrier_name = ''. That is an empty string, not NULL, so it passes any “is it filled in?” check.

17 names match two carriers at once, e.g. Smart Track Royalmail 2nd 100g LL — is that Smart Track, or Royal Mail? These 17 names cover 197,227 labels, so the answer materially changes per-carrier spend.

Two label types are hiding in the carrier column: collection order (144) and Return 2kg Hermes (137). Those are not carriers.
